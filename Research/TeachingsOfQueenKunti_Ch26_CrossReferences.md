## Overview of the Chapter

Chapter 26 of *Teachings of Queen Kuntī* (TQK) is an expanded commentary on **Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.8.43**, Kuntīdevī's prayer to Kṛṣṇa:

> *śrī-kṛṣṇa kṛṣṇa-sakha vṛṣṇy-ṛṣabhāvani-dhrug-rājanya-vaṁśa-dahanānapavarga-vīrya govinda go-dvija-surārti-harāvatāra yogeśvarākhila-guro bhagavan namas te*
>
> "O Kṛṣṇa, O friend of Arjuna, O chief among the descendants of Vṛṣṇi, You are the destroyer of those political parties which are disturbing elements on this earth. Your prowess never deteriorates. You are the proprietor of the transcendental abode, and You descend to relieve the distresses of the cows, the brāhmaṇas, and the devotees. You possess all mystic powers, and You are the preceptor of the entire universe. You are the almighty God, and I offer You my respectful obeisances."

The chapter title — "Enchantment by Kṛṣṇa's Glories" — reflects Prabhupāda's close unpacking of each epithet in this densely packed prayer. The chapter develops five interlocking themes:

1. **Pavarga and apavarga** — the Sanskrit linguistic analysis of the word *pavarga* (the letters *pa, pha, ba, bha, ma*) as a map of material suffering (hard labor, foam at the mouth, frustration, fear, death), and *anapavarga* as that which negates this path — Kṛṣṇa's unfailing power to liberate.

2. **Governance as divine representation** — kings and presidents are called *naradeva* ("God in human form") because they are meant to be Kṛṣṇa's representatives; when they rebel and claim supremacy themselves, Kṛṣṇa descends to annihilate them (*rājanya-vaṁśa-dahana*).

3. **Kṛṣṇa as *Govinda* and *ādi-puruṣa*** — the original person who maintains surabhi cows in Golokadhāma, served by hundreds of thousands of goddesses of fortune; the source of all demigods.

4. **The spiritual master as sevaka-bhagavān** — drawing on Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's verse and SB 11.17.27, Prabhupāda distinguishes between the *sevya-bhagavān* (Kṛṣṇa, who is worshiped) and the *sevaka-bhagavān* (the spiritual master, who is the worshiper).

5. **Kṛṣṇa kathā and the brahma-bhūta stage** — the second half of the chapter, in lecture format, explains how hearing the Bhagavad-gītā (Kṛṣṇa's words) and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (words about Kṛṣṇa) cleanses the heart of material contamination and raises the soul through the modes to the *brahma-bhūta* platform of pure consciousness, free from hankering and lamentation.

### 1. Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.8.43 — The Root Verse

The entire chapter is Prabhupāda's purport to this verse, expanded from the compact SB purport. The SB purport closes: "The cows, the brāhmaṇas and the devotees of the Lord are all objects of His special attention because they are very important factors for the general welfare of living beings." TQK Ch.26 opens with the same summary but then extends it into a full-length lecture on each epithet. Comparing the SB purport directly with the chapter text shows the expansion process: each phrase in the purport becomes a paragraph or section in the TQK commentary.

### 2. Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 7.13.25 — The *Pa-varga* Analysis

The chapter's most distinctive textual feature is a detailed linguistic commentary on the word *pavarga*. Prabhupāda explains that *pavarga* refers to the five Sanskrit consonants *pa, pha, ba, bha, ma*, which stand respectively for *pariśrama* (hard labor), *phena* (foaming at the mouth from exhaustion), *vyarthatā* (futility or frustration), *bhaya* (fear), and *mṛtyu* (death) — in other words, the entire arc of material suffering. *Anapavarga* negates all five; therefore *anapavarga-vīrya* means that Kṛṣṇa's prowess leads souls entirely out of this cycle.

This exact same analysis — including all five letter-correspondences — appears in Prabhupāda's purport to **SB 7.13.25**, where he writes:

> "Material life is called *pavarga* because here we are subject to five different states of suffering, represented by the letters *pa, pha, ba, bha* and *ma*. *Pa* means *pariśrama*, very hard labor. *Pha* means *phena*, or foam from the mouth. … *Ba* means *byarthatā*, disappointment. … *Bha* means *bhaya*, or fear. … Finally, *ma* means *mṛtyu*, or death. When one attempts to nullify these five … one achieves *apavarga*, or liberation."

The SB 7.13.25 purport adds that the human form is a gateway to either *svarga* (elevation to higher planets), *apavarga* (return to Godhead), or *tiraścām* (animal births). It also quotes Bg 3.27 (*prakṛteḥ kriyamāṇāni*) in the same context, identical to TQK Ch.26's use of the modes as the mechanism of material bondage.

### 3. Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Madhya-līlā 22.84 and 22.86 — *Bhavāpavarga* Through Devotee Association

The word *apavarga* in a devotional context — liberation from wandering — is the theological anchor of **Cc. Madhya 22.84**, which quotes SB 10.51.53:

> *bhavāpavargo bhramato yadā bhavej janasya tarhy acyuta sat-samāgamaḥ*
> "O infallible Lord! When a person wandering throughout the universes becomes eligible for liberation from material existence, he gets an opportunity to associate with devotees."

This verse defines *bhavāpavarga* as the grace that opens when a soul is ready: not through personal effort alone but through *sat-saṅga*, saintly association. It directly complements TQK Ch.26's argument that Kṛṣṇa (*anapavarga-vīrya*) is the agent of liberation — not impersonal yoga, but devotional service initiated by hearing from devotees.

**Cc. Madhya 22.86** (quoting SB 3.25.25) adds:

> *satāṁ prasaṅgān mama vīrya-saṁvido bhavanti hṛt-karṇa-rasāyanāḥ kathāḥ taj-joṣaṇād āśv apavarga-vartmani śraddhā ratir bhaktir anukramiṣyati*
> "The spiritually powerful message of Godhead … quickly opens the way of transcendental experience, and gradually one attains firm faith that in due course develops into attraction and devotion."

The phrase *apavarga-vartmani* here — the path to *apavarga* — mirrors the chapter's argument that Kṛṣṇa's prowess (*vīrya*) is specifically described as *anapavarga* because only He can open this path.

### 4. Bhagavad-gītā 10.2 — Kṛṣṇa as the Origin of All Demigods

TQK Ch.26 addresses Kṛṣṇa's epithet *Govinda* and his status as *ādi-puruṣa* (the original person). Prabhupāda cites **Bg 10.2** inline — *aham ādir hi devānām* ("I am the origin even of these demigods") — to rebuke those who think Brahmā, Viṣṇu, or Śiva is the ultimate origin. The purport to Bg 10.2 elaborates that even the great demigods cannot understand Kṛṣṇa's nature by mental speculation, and that the impersonalists who conclude God must be formless are limited by material reasoning:

> "Here the Lord indirectly says … 'I am the Supreme Personality of Godhead. I am the Supreme.' … The devotees who are fully surrendered … can understand, by the grace of the Supreme Personality, that He is Kṛṣṇa."

This is the same philosophical point TQK Ch.26 makes against the Māyāvādīs: because God is unlimited and we are limited, they reason He must be impersonal — but that is a material comparison (like thinking an unlimited sky is impersonal), not the Vedic teaching.

### 5. Brahma-saṁhitā 5.29 — *Govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ* (via Cc. Ādi 5.22)

TQK Ch.26 cites the *Brahma-saṁhitā* refrain *govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi* (marked `**` in the VedaBase plain text) twice to establish that Kṛṣṇa — Govinda — is the original person worshiped in Golokadhāma, tending surabhi cows and served by goddesses of fortune. The verse appears in full at **Cc. Ādi 5.22**:

> *cintāmaṇi-prakara-sadmasu kalpa-vṛkṣa-lakṣāvṛteṣu surabhīr abhipālayantam lakṣmī-sahasra-śata-sambhrama-sevyamānaṁ govindam ādi-puruṣaṁ tam ahaṁ bhajāmi*
> "I worship Govinda, the primeval Lord, the first progenitor, who is tending cows yielding all desires in abodes built with spiritual gems …"

The Cc. Ādi 5.22 purport explains that Kṛṣṇaloka is the topmost planet in the spiritual sky, where the modes of nature have no influence and everything is eternal, blissful, and full of knowledge — the destination pointed to in TQK Ch.26 as the goal of *apavarga*.

### 6. Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.17.5 and 9.15.38 — *Naradeva*: The King as God's Representative

TQK Ch.26 explains at length that the Sanskrit term *naradeva* ("God in human form") is the basis for royal authority: a king is honored above ordinary citizens because his duty is to represent Kṛṣṇa. When kings rebel and treat their kingdoms as personal property, claiming "I am God," Kṛṣṇa descends to annihilate them (*avani-dhrug-rājanya-vaṁśa-dahana*).

**SB 1.17.5** (Mahārāja Parīkṣit confronting Kali) encodes the same doctrine: a true *nara-deva* is one who is *sarva-deva-mayam* — the embodiment of all the demigods — by virtue of protecting the helpless. The purport notes that in Kali-yuga administrators are "man-gods by dress or office, but factually worthless, lower-class men without the cultural assets of the twice-born."

**SB 9.15.38** provides the complementary warning: Paraśurāma's mother says that even killing a *naradeva* (a king), because "he is the embodiment of all the demigods," constitutes a sin — a statement that shows how seriously the Vedic tradition took the king's representative status.

### 7. Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 11.17.27 and Viśvanātha Cakravartī Ṭhākura's Gurv-aṣṭaka — The Spiritual Master as *Sevaka-bhagavān*

A substantial section of TQK Ch.26 addresses the false spiritual master who claims to be God. Prabhupāda draws a precise theological distinction using Viśvanātha Cakravartī's verse (*sākṣād-dharitvena samasta-śāstrair uktaḥ* — `**` in the VedaBase text): the spiritual master is to be honored *as* Kṛṣṇa (*sevaka-bhagavān*, the worshiper-God), but is not Kṛṣṇa the worshipped (*sevya-bhagavān*). One who claims *sevya* status is a Māyāvādī rebel.

This passage is embedded in **SB 11.17.27**, where Kṛṣṇa instructs Uddhava:

> *ācāryaṁ māṁ vijānīyān nāvamanyeta karhicit na martya-buddhyāsūyeta sarva-devamayo guruḥ*
> "One should know the ācārya as Myself and never disrespect him in any way … for he is the representative of all the demigods."

Prabhupāda's lengthy SB 11.17.27 purport (itself quoting Cc. Ādi 1.46) develops the same *sevya/sevaka* distinction TQK Ch.26 makes: the spiritual master "is not an enjoyer of facilities offered by his disciples … He is like a parent." Crucially, the purport adds that "if one poses himself as an ācārya but does not have an attitude of servitorship to the Lord, he must be considered an offender."

### 8. Bhagavad-gītā 18.61 — Kṛṣṇa Seated in Every Heart

TQK Ch.26 cites **Bg 18.61** (*Īśvaraḥ sarva-bhūtānāṁ hṛd deśe 'rjuna tiṣṭhati*) to make the point that Kṛṣṇa is never absent — He accompanies the soul through every birth and waits for the soul to turn to Him: "He is so kind that though we may forget Him, He never forgets us." The Bg 18.61 purport in the same passage explains that the Supersoul "sits in the heart directing the living being" through all changes of body, and that the living entity is "not independent" — always under the Lord's control.

This verse appears in TQK Ch.26 immediately before the teaching on *buddhi-yoga*: once one understands that the supreme consciousness (Kṛṣṇa) is always present and directing, one can surrender all activities to Him and transcend the reactions of pious and impious work.

### 9. Bhagavad-gītā 18.54 — The *Brahma-bhūta* Stage

The final sustained argument of TQK Ch.26 explains how hearing Kṛṣṇa kathā progressively lifts the soul out of the modes of ignorance and passion, through the mode of goodness, to the *brahma-bhūta* state. At that stage "one will find oneself joyful in every circumstance of life" and "will have no hankering and no lamentation." The chapter marks this with the reference "[SB 4.30.20]," but the definitive description of *brahma-bhūta* is **Bg 18.54**:

> *brahma-bhūtaḥ prasannātmā na śocati na kāṅkṣati samaḥ sarveṣu bhūteṣu mad-bhaktiṁ labhate parām*
> "One who is thus transcendentally situated … never laments or desires to have anything. He is equally disposed toward every living entity. In that state he attains pure devotional service unto Me."

Prabhupāda's Bg 18.54 purport clarifies that *brahma-bhūta* is not the end for the devotee — it is the platform from which pure devotional service (*mad-bhaktiṁ labhate parām*) begins. TQK Ch.26 reinforces the same point: *brahma-bhūta* is situated in the mode of goodness, but it must continue as devotional activity (*buddhi-yoga*) to achieve complete liberation from birth and death.

### 10. Bhagavad-gītā 18.66 — Full Surrender as *Apavarga*

TQK Ch.26 ends its purport section with Kṛṣṇa's promise in **Bg 18.66** (*ahaṁ tvāṁ sarva-pāpebhyo mokṣayiṣyāmi*), cited inline as "[Bg. 18.66]": "Surrender unto Me. I shall give you *apavarga*." The juxtaposition is deliberate — having explained that *anapavarga-vīrya* means Kṛṣṇa's prowess leads to the negation of all five material tribulations, Prabhupāda points to Bg 18.66 as the operative mechanism: unconditional surrender is the means, and Kṛṣṇa's unfailing protection (*mokṣayiṣyāmi*) is the result.

The Bg 18.66 purport adds that "one who surrenders unto Kṛṣṇa does not have to execute so many methods" — negating the need for elaborate yoga, jñāna, or karma-mīmāṁsā. This matches TQK Ch.26's argument that the entire *pavarga* path (hard labor, fear, death) is bypassed in one step.

### 11. Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam 1.2.18 — *Nityaṁ Bhāgavata-sevayā* (Hearing Cleanses the Heart)

The lecture portion of TQK Ch.26 is a sustained exegesis of *Kṛṣṇa kathā* — the two classes of transcendental topics: Bhagavad-gītā (spoken *by* Kṛṣṇa) and Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam (spoken *about* Kṛṣṇa). Prabhupāda argues that regular hearing of either works to dissolve "all inauspicious things within our hearts … accumulated life after life." This is precisely the teaching of **SB 1.2.18**:

> *naṣṭa-prāyeṣv abhadreṣu nityaṁ bhāgavata-sevayā bhagavaty uttama-śloke bhaktir bhavati naiṣṭhikī*
> "By regular attendance in classes on the Bhāgavatam and by rendering of service to the pure devotee, all that is troublesome to the heart is almost completely destroyed, and loving service … is established as an irrevocable fact."

The SB 1.2.18 purport introduces the distinction between the two types of *Bhāgavata*: the book Bhāgavata (*Śrīmad-Bhāgavatam*) and the person Bhāgavata (the devotee). Both are "competent remedies" for eliminating heart-contamination. TQK Ch.26's teaching that "simply by hearing, one acquires some piety" and that the vibration of Kṛṣṇa kathā is "pure transcendental vibration" maps exactly onto SB 1.2.18's promise.

### 12. Caitanya-caritāmṛta, Ādi-līlā 3.40 — The Holy Name as Kṛṣṇa's Avatāra in Kali-yuga

TQK Ch.26 opens its lecture section by identifying the Hare Kṛṣṇa mahā-mantra as Kṛṣṇa's specific avatāra for this age: "In the present age, Kali-yuga, people are very much sinful … Therefore Kṛṣṇa has incarnated in the form of His name." This is a standard Gauḍīya Vaiṣṇava teaching, most concisely stated in **Cc. Ādi 3.40**:

> *kali-yuge yuga-dharma—nāmera pracāra tathi lāgi' pīta-varṇa caitanyāvatāra*
> "The religious practice for the Age of Kali is to broadcast the glories of the holy name. Only for this purpose has the Lord, in a yellow color, descended as Lord Caitanya."

Prabhupāda's Cc. Ādi 3.40 purport cites the *Kali-santaraṇa Upaniṣad* to confirm that the sixteen-name mantra (*Hare Kṛṣṇa…*) is prescribed by all the Vedas as the supreme method for this age — supporting TQK Ch.26's claim that chanting and hearing the holy names of Kṛṣṇa is "as good as bodily association with Kṛṣṇa Himself."

He then develops the same points about *akāma* (desireless devotees) versus *sarva-kāma* (materialists who want endless gifts), which parallels TQK Ch.26's teaching on how the *brahma-bhūta* stage requires abandoning material hankering.

### 14. Bhagavad-gītā 3.8 — Even the Body Requires Work (*Pavarga* Context)

TQK Ch.26 cites **Bg 3.8** (*śarīra-yātrāpi ca te na prasiddhyed akarmaṇaḥ* — "one cannot even maintain one's own body without work") in the context of the letter *pa* for *pariśrama* (hard labor): "Kṛṣṇa never advised Arjuna, 'I am your friend, and I shall do everything. You just sit down and smoke gañjā.'" This establishes that even in this world of *pavarga*, work is unavoidable — the question is whether it is performed in the mode of consciousness (*buddhi-yoga*) or in bodily identification. The citation connects the *pavarga* analysis directly to the Gītā's doctrine of *niṣkāma-karma*.

### 15. Īśopaniṣad, Mantra 1 — Everything Belongs to God

TQK Ch.26 cites **Īśo mantra 1** (*īśāvāsyam idaṁ sarvam*) inline as "[Īśo mantra 1]" in the discussion of rebellious kings: "The fact is that everything belongs to God." A king who says "This is my kingdom" is making the same error as the Māyāvādī who claims to be God — both are denying Kṛṣṇa's ownership. Prabhupāda uses the Upaniṣadic mantra to ground the political-theological argument in śruti authority: the legitimacy of a king, like the legitimacy of a spiritual master, depends entirely on his representing the actual proprietor.
