## The Verse Itself

**Bhagavad-gita 4.15** (Transcendental Knowledge)

**Sanskrit (Devanagari — transliteration):**

```
evaṁ jñātvā kṛtaṁ karma
pūrvair api mumukṣubhiḥ
kuru karmaiva tasmāt tvaṁ
pūrvaiḥ pūrva-taraṁ kṛtam
```

**Word-for-word (BBT final edition):**

> evam — thus; jñātvā — knowing well; kṛtam — was performed; karma — work; pūrvaiḥ — by past authorities; api — indeed; mumukṣubhiḥ — who attained liberation; kuru — just perform; karma — prescribed duty; eva — certainly; tasmāt — therefore; tvam — you; pūrvaiḥ — by the predecessors; pūrva-taram — in ancient times; kṛtam — as performed.

**Translation (BBT final edition):**

> All the liberated souls in ancient times acted with this understanding of My transcendental nature. Therefore you should perform your duty, following in their footsteps.

**Translation (1972 MacMillan edition — variant):**

> All the liberated souls in ancient times acted with this understanding and so attained liberation. Therefore, as the ancients, you should perform your duty in this divine consciousness.

**Central theme:** Acting in devotional service following the example of previous authorities (*pūrvaiḥ*), understanding Krishna's transcendental nature, as the means to liberation.

## Srila Prabhupada — Purport

*Source type: Book (highest authority in Srila Prabhupada's SAC hierarchy)*

### BBT Final Edition Purport

**Source:** Bhagavad-gita As It Is, Chapter 4, Text 15 (final BBT edition)

> There are two classes of men. Some of them are full of polluted material things within their hearts, and some of them are materially free. Kṛṣṇa consciousness is equally beneficial for both of these persons. Those who are full of dirty things can take to the line of Kṛṣṇa consciousness for a gradual cleansing process, following the regulative principles of devotional service. Those who are already cleansed of the impurities may continue to act in the same Kṛṣṇa consciousness so that others may follow their exemplary activities and thereby be benefited. Foolish persons or neophytes in Kṛṣṇa consciousness often want to retire from activities without having knowledge of Kṛṣṇa consciousness. Arjuna's desire to retire from activities on the battlefield was not approved by the Lord. One need only know how to act. To retire from the activities of Kṛṣṇa consciousness and sit aloof making a show of Kṛṣṇa consciousness is less important than actually engaging in the field of activities for the sake of Kṛṣṇa. Arjuna is here advised to act in Kṛṣṇa consciousness, following in the footsteps of the Lord's previous disciples, such as the sun-god Vivasvān, as mentioned hereinbefore. The Supreme Lord knows all His past activities, as well as those of persons who acted in Kṛṣṇa consciousness in the past. Therefore He recommends the acts of the sun-god, who learned this art from the Lord some millions of years before. All such students of Lord Kṛṣṇa are mentioned here as past liberated persons, engaged in the discharge of duties allotted by Kṛṣṇa.

**Key points from the purport:**

1. Two classes of people benefit from Krishna consciousness: (a) those whose hearts are polluted — Krishna consciousness cleanses them gradually; (b) those already purified — their continued action in Krishna consciousness sets an example for others.
2. Neophytes wrongly want to retire from activity; Arjuna's desire to leave the battlefield was rejected.
3. Active engagement in Krishna's service is more important than sitting aloof in a show of Krishna consciousness.
4. Arjuna is advised to follow previous disciples like Vivasvān the sun-god.
5. The "past liberated persons" referred to are all previous students of Lord Krishna who engaged in duties allotted by Krishna.

### 1972 MacMillan Edition Purport

**Source:** Bhagavad-gita As It Is, 1972 edition, Chapter 4, Text 15

The 1972 purport text is substantively identical to the BBT final edition. The only differences are minor punctuation and one slight phrasing change: the 1972 edition reads "to sit aloof making a show of Kṛṣṇa consciousness;" (with a semicolon instead of a comma after "consciousness"). The substance is the same.

The 1972 edition synonyms show slight variants: *pūrvaiḥ — by past authorities; api — although; mumukṣubhiḥ — who attained liberation; pūrvaiḥ — by the predecessors; pūrvataram — ancient predecessors; kṛtam — as performed.* (Note: "pūrva-taram" is unhyphenated "pūrvataram" in 1972; "api" is translated as "although" rather than "indeed.")

## Srila Prabhupada — Lectures

*Source type: Lecture (second tier in SAC hierarchy)*

### Bhagavad-gita 4.14–19, New York, August 1, 1966

**File code:** 660801BG.NY | Duration: 61:57 minutes
**Index title:** "Follow the example of the previous ācāryas"

Srila Prabhupada reads verse 4.15 and discusses it as part of a continuous lecture on BG 4.14–19. Key passages on verse 4.15:

> *evaṁ jñātvā kṛtaṁ karma pūrvair api mumukṣubhiḥ kuru karmaiva tasmāt tvaṁ pūrvaiḥ pūrvataraṁ kṛtam* [Bg 4.15]
>
> Now, the whole spiritual process is to follow the example of the previous ācāryas who have attained, I mean to..., success.

> Kṛṣṇa is advising Arjuna that "If you act and follow in the footprints of the previous ācāryas and previous great devotees and kings who have done in their life karma-yoga, acting for Kṛṣṇa, if we follow that principle, then you shall also become free from the reaction of activities." Because Arjuna was very much afraid for being entangled in the reaction of his fighting, Kṛṣṇa therefore assures that "You shall not be... If you follow, if you act, if you fight for My sake, then you will not be entangled by the reaction of karma."

The lecture continues through verses 4.16–4.19, explaining karma, akarma, and vikarma in detail.

### Bhagavad-gita 4.15, Bombay, April 4, 1974

**File code:** 740404BG.BOM | Duration: 36:29 minutes
**Index title:** "Modern Civilization Vs. Model Civilization"

This is the only lecture specifically dedicated to BG 4.15 in the VedaBase corpus. It was delivered by Srila Prabhupada in Bombay. Key passages:

**On the word *pūrva*, *pūrvataram*, *pūrvatama*:**

> Now, in this verse three times, four times, the word has been used, pūrvataram, pūrvatamaiḥ. *Kuru karmaiva tasmāt tvaṁ pūrvaiḥ pūrvataraṁ kṛtam.* Pūrva, pūrvataram, pūrvatama: comparative, superlative and positive. So this is the process that we have to follow the previous ācāryas. That is Vedic system. In the very beginning of this chapter Kṛṣṇa said, *evaṁ paramparā-prāptam imaṁ rājarṣayo viduḥ* [Bg 4.2].
>
> We haven't got to make any research, modern understanding. There is no question of modern understanding. We are all following the old, ancient understanding.

**On modern civilization vs. the Vedic model:**

> So it is useless to say that modern time, modern ways, modern things. There is nothing modern. The old thing is going on. So if we follow systematically the previous ācāryas, previous system, as Kṛṣṇa is advising... Kṛṣṇa does not advise, "Modern." Five thousand years ago He instructed Bhagavad-gītā. Still, He was saying pūrva pūrvataraiḥ, pūrvatamaiḥ. *Evaṁ paramparā-prāptam imaṁ rājarṣayo viduḥ* [Bg 4.2]. This is intelligence.

**On the meaning of *mumukṣubhiḥ* (seekers of liberation):**

> So this is Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement, that everyone should be employed in Kṛṣṇa consciousness... This kind of business can be done by whom? Mumukṣubhiḥ, not ordinary men. Those who are aspiring after liberation. Those who are thinking of stopping this nonsense business of repetition of birth and death, for them. They are not ordinary men. Mumukṣubhiḥ.

**On following Arjuna as an example:**

> So the śāstra says... Mahājano yena gataḥ sa panthāḥ [Cc Madhya 17.186]... "Pūrvataraiḥ. Just Arjuna did, Arjuna accepted, accept like that." Then your Bhagavad-gītā reading is perfect. Otherwise simply wasting your time and misleading others... Pūrvataraiḥ. So the śāstra says, there is no misinterpretation. Try to understand Bhagavad-gītā as it is. Your life will be successful.

**On the catur-varnya connection:**

> So Kṛṣṇa is advising here, *evaṁ jñātvā kṛtaṁ karma pūrvair api mumukṣubhiḥ.* Evaṁ jñātvā. First of all it is the business of brāhmaṇa to understand. So if you cannot understand, then you do the business of kṣatriya. If you cannot do that, then do the business of a vaiśya. And if you cannot do that, then remain as a worker, assist others, assist the brāhmaṇa, the kṣatriya. So everyone will be engaged.

**On following Kṛṣṇa's instruction:**

> So Kṛṣṇa is advising, *kuru karmaiva tasmāt tvam.* That is advice of Kṛṣṇa to Arjuna, that "You follow the previous ācāryas, previous personalities, authorities," just like svayambhūr nāradaḥ śambhuḥ... "You follow them, and act like that, and you try to understand that what is My position. If you simply understand My position, immediately you become liberated." *Janma karma me divyaṁ yo jānāti tattvataḥ, tyaktvā dehaṁ punar janma naiti.* This is liberation.

## Srila Prabhupada — Other Books

*Source type: Book (same tier as BG As It Is)*

### Raja Vidya, Chapter 8: Action in Knowledge of Kṛṣṇa

**Source:** *Raja Vidya: The King of Knowledge*, Chapter 8

Raja Vidya chapter 8 quotes BG 4.15 immediately after explaining BG 4.14, as part of a teaching on how to work without karmic reaction:

> Although the Supreme Lord is full and has nothing to do, He works in order to set an example. He is not bound to His activities in the material world, and one who knows this also becomes free from reactional activities.
>
> *evaṁ jñātvā kṛtaṁ karma pūrvair api mumukṣubhiḥ kuru karmaiva tasmāt tvaṁ pūrvaiḥ pūrvataraṁ kṛtam*
>
> "All the liberated souls in ancient times acted with this understanding and so attained liberation. Therefore, as the ancients, you should perform your duty in this divine consciousness." (Bg. 4.15)
>
> The process of Kṛṣṇa consciousness requires that we follow in the footsteps of the great ācāryas who have attained success in spiritual life. If one acts by following the examples set by great ācāryas, sages, devotees and enlightened kings who have performed karma-yoga in their lives, he shall also become free.
>
> On the battlefield of Kurukṣetra, Arjuna was very much afraid of being entangled in his activities by engaging in warfare. Kṛṣṇa therefore assured him that if he fought for His sake there would be no possibility of entanglement.

The chapter continues with BG 4.16, explaining what constitutes karma, vikarma, and akarma.

## Other Vaishnava Sources

### Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura

**Work:** *Sārārtha-Varṣiṇī* (Commentary on Bhagavad-gita)
**Source file:** `gita_04_gyana_yoga.html` (Nitaai Veda corpus)

Srila Visvanatha Cakravarti Thakura's brief Sanskrit commentary (*tika*) on BG 4.15, as translated in the companion text:

> "Previous authorities such as Janaka, knowing Me in this way, have also performed karma to set an ideal for humanity."

This emphasizes *pūrvaiḥ* (previous authorities, specifically citing King Janaka) and identifies their karma-yoga as model behavior meant to establish the ideal for common people.

### Srila Bhaktivedanta Narayana Maharaja

**Work:** *Sārārtha-Varṣiṇī Prakāśikā-Vṛtti* (sub-commentary on the Sarartha-Varsini)
**Author:** Sri Srimad Bhaktivedanta Narayana Maharaja
**Source file:** `gita_04_gyana_yoga.html` (Nitaai Veda corpus)

Translation provided in this commentary:

> Even the mumukshus (seekers of liberation) in ancient times, who understood My tattva in this way, performed their prescribed duties simply to set the standard for common men. Therefore, you should also perform your karma, following the example of these predecessor authorities.

**Word-for-word from this edition:**

> evam — thus; jnatva — knowing; api — even; purvaih — the ancients; mumukshubhih — who desired liberation; kritam — performed; karma — work; eva — certainly; tasmat — therefore; tvam — you; kuru — must perform; karma — the action; kritam — performed; purvataram — previously; purvaih — by those ancients.

The commentary in the following verse (4.16) clarifies an important point about this instruction: "Even the wise should not perform karma simply by imitating the previous acaryas. Rather, they should act only when they have understood the specific nature of karma (action)." This indicates that *pūrvataraiḥ* does not mean mechanical imitation but understanding before acting.

### Srila Madhvacharya

**Work:** *Gītā-bhāṣyam* (Bhashya on Bhagavad-gita by Sri Ananda Tirtha — Madhvacharya)
**Source file:** `gitabhashyam.html` (Nitaai Veda corpus)

The Gitabhashyam lists verse 4.15 in IAST within the continuous text of chapter 4. The verse text as given:

```
evam jnatva krtam karma purvairapi mumuksubhih |
kuru karmaiva tasmattvam purvaih purvataram krtam || 4.15||
```

The Gitabhashyam corpus file available contains the complete chapter 4 verse texts but the Sanskrit prose commentary (*bhashya*) on individual verses is not separately extracted for 4.15 in the available HTML format. The verse appears in proper sequence between 4.14 and 4.16. Madhvacharya's tradition interprets this verse in the Dvaita framework — that work performed with knowledge of Vishnu's transcendence frees the soul — consistent with its Vaishnava reading across all schools.
