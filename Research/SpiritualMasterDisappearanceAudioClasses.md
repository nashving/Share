## Background and Scope

Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura, Prabhupada's diksha (initiating) spiritual master, departed from this world on **December 31, 1936**, in Puri, Orissa. Because the Vaishnava observance calendar follows lunar tithis, the anniversary falls on a different Gregorian date each year — ranging from early December to late December in the years covered by the transcripts. The designator "DA" or "DB" in audio filenames stands for "Disappearance Anniversary" (Bhaktisiddhanta).

The search covered folder 11 (chronological audio transcripts 1966–1977) and folder 12 (by subject/verse). Folder 12 yielded no additional unique files; all significant material is in folder 11. All sources are **audio transcripts — Rank 3 (spoken; occasion-tied)** per the source hierarchy.

## Question 1: Which Classes Mention the Disappearance?

Fourteen classes in total. The four dedicated disappearance day lectures (entries 1–4) are the most concentrated sources. The three appearance day lectures (entries 5–7) also treat the disappearance at length because they recount Prabhupada's personal history with Bhaktisiddhanta, which inevitably includes his passing. The incidental references (entries 8–14) arise in various contexts — planning an observance, the day after the festival, recounting personal history during an unrelated lecture — and vary in depth from a single passing sentence to several paragraphs.

## Question 2: What Does He Say About the Event and Its Significance?

### The Disappearance as Non-Different from Appearance

The most consistent theological point, stated in almost every class, is that the disappearance of a Vaishnava ācārya is not like ordinary death. Prabhupada uses the Sanskrit technical terms *prakaṭa* (manifest) and *aprakaṭa* (unmanifest), or *āvirbhāva* (appearance) and *tirobhāva* (disappearance):

> "So there are two phases: prakaṭa and aprakaṭa, appearance and disappearance. So we have nothing to lament on account of disappearance because Kṛṣṇa and Kṛṣṇa's devotees... nobody disappears." — December 13, 1973, Los Angeles

> "Just like the sun rises at six o'clock, and seven o'clock there is six o'clock in another place... it is going on, nitya-līlā. So we have nothing to lament for disappearance. We should simply remember his activities." — February 8, 1977, Mayapur

He illustrates the same point with the analogy of the sun: its "setting" is a defect of our eyes, not the end of the sun's existence. Applied to the ācārya:

> "So Tirobhāva-Āvirbhāva they are the same thing as the appearance and disappearance of sun. And Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura, our master, spiritual master, he also came in this world to execute some mission... and when it was required, he left this place and went to another place to do the same business." — February 8, 1977, Mayapur

In the October 17, 1977 Room Conversation — made within weeks of Prabhupada's own passing — he extended the same principle to himself when his disciples expressed reluctance about planning a Hyderabad temple opening on the disappearance day:

> Girirāja: "Except in your case. We don't want disappearance."
> Prabhupāda: "[chuckles] Jīvo vā māro vā. Because we are going to accept eternity, so appearance and disappearance of this body is not a very important thing. Nitya-līlā, eternal life — that is our Kṛṣṇa consciousness." — October 17, 1977, Vrindavan

### Genuine Separation and Grief

Despite the theological framework of non-difference, Prabhupada also expressed authentic grief and separation. In the December 13, 1973 lecture in Los Angeles he quoted the lamentation song of Narottama Das Thakura — *ye anilo prema-dhana / karuṇā pracura / heno prabhu kothā gelo* ("Where has gone that lord who brought the treasure of divine love in such abundance?") — and noted that he could not recall the full text. He then said:

> "That is our lamentation, that Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura brought this message to distribute all over..." — December 13, 1973, Los Angeles

In the same lecture he began to cry when thanking his disciples for helping him carry out Bhaktisiddhanta's order:

> "So this is a memorable day. What he desired, I am trying little bit, and you are all helping me. So I have to thank you more. You are actually representative of my Guru Mahārāja [starts to cry] ...because you are helping me in executing the order of my Guru Mahārāja." — December 13, 1973, Los Angeles

### The Day as an Occasion for Renewed Commitment

Each disappearance day lecture uses the anniversary as an occasion for Prabhupada to call his disciples to continued effort. The 1968 Los Angeles lecture is the clearest statement of this:

> "Now on this occasion of my spiritual master's departure, as I am trying to execute his will, similarly, I shall also request you to execute the same order through my will. I am an old man. I can also pass away at any moment... my appeal to you on this auspicious day of the departure of my Guru Mahārāja, that at least to some extent you have understood the essence of Kṛṣṇa consciousness movement. You should try to push it on." — December 9, 1968, Los Angeles

### Bhaktisiddhanta's Special Contribution

Prabhupada consistently identifies Bhaktisiddhanta's specific historical contribution as the aggressive, organized, worldwide preaching mission — in contrast to the previous Gosvāmīs who left literature but did not preach publicly in the West:

> "Śrī Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura's special contribution... he was very, very anxious to preach this Caitanya cult in the Western countries. Before that, even the ācāryas, Rūpānuga, Gosvāmīs, they left literature, but they did not attempt to preach practically." — December 13, 1973, Los Angeles

He also credited Bhaktisiddhanta with defeating pseudo-Vaishnava caste gosvamis who had corrupted the tradition:

> "My Guru Mahārāja's contribution is that he defeated these caste gosvāmī... just according to the rules and regulation of Caitanya Mahāprabhu." — February 7, 1969, Los Angeles

## Question 3: Final Instruction Received at or Near the Time of Disappearance

This is the most consistently narrated element across the whole corpus. Prabhupada recounts it in nearly identical form in at least five classes across fifteen years (1968, 1969, 1973, 1974, 1976).

### The Sequence of Events

In the final months of 1936, Bhaktisiddhanta was seriously ill and staying at Jagannatha Puri. Prabhupada was then a householder in Bombay. He wrote to his Guru Maharaja expressing his sorrow at not being able to render direct service like the brahmacaris and sannyasis in the Math:

> "My dear master, your other disciples, brahmacārī, sannyāsī, they are rendering you direct service. And I am a householder. I cannot live with you, I cannot serve you nicely. So I do not know how can I serve you." — Prabhupada's description, December 9, 1968, Los Angeles

The reply, dated **December 13, 1936**, came from Puri. Bhaktisiddhanta wrote:

> "My dear such and such, I am very glad to receive your letter. I think you should try to push our movement in English. And that will do good to you and to the people who will help you." — Cited by Prabhupada, December 9, 1968, Los Angeles

Bhaktisiddhanta then passed away on **December 31, 1936** — just a fortnight (two weeks) after writing the letter. Prabhupada describes this repeatedly:

> "And then in 1936... he passed away on the 31st December. Just a fortnight before [he had written to me]." — February 7, 1969, Los Angeles

> "He passed away on 31st December, 1936. So almost forty years past." — December 13, 1973, Los Angeles (opening of the lecture)

> "Then at the end of his life in 1936, he wrote me a letter, fifteen days before his disappearance, that you preach in English; in this you will be benefitted and even the world will be benefitted." — February 13, 1974, Vrindavan (Hindi lecture)

### The 1922 First Meeting as the Origin of the Instruction

Prabhupada consistently frames the December 1936 letter not as a new instruction but as the final reaffirmation of what Bhaktisiddhanta had said at their very first meeting in 1922. The pattern is stated explicitly in the 1976 Hyderabad lecture, delivered forty years after the letter:

> "So whatever I appreciated forty years ago, the same principle is going on. There is nothing new... In 1922 I met my Guru Mahārāja... And his first opening version was that 'You are educated young men. Why don't you preach Caitanya Mahāprabhu's gospel in the Western countries?'" — December 10, 1976, Hyderabad

The 1976 lecture was delivered with Prabhupada reading from a poem he had composed for Bhaktisiddhanta's Vyasa-puja in 1936 — the same year as the final letter and the departure. He notes that his Godbrothers had recently rediscovered the poem in a London museum copy of *The Harmonist* (the Gaudiya Math journal):

> "I thought the poetry is lost, but I do not know how it was found out by some of our disciples... from the Harmonist, my Guru Mahārāja's paper, this poetry was found." — December 10, 1976, Hyderabad

### Dreams as Ongoing Instruction After the Departure

In several classes, Prabhupada speaks of dreams of Bhaktisiddhanta calling him even after the departure. These are described as the mechanism by which Bhaktisiddhanta pressed him toward sannyasa and Western preaching:

> "Sometimes I was dreaming of my spiritual master, that he's calling me, and I was following him. When my dream was over, I was thinking — I was little horrified — 'Oh, Guru Mahārāja wants me to become sannyāsī. How can I accept sannyāsa?'... I saw several times, calling me." — October 21, 1968, Seattle (Bhaktiprajnana Kesava Maharaja Disappearance Day)

> "I was dreaming that Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Ṭhākura is calling me, 'Please come out with me!'... [I was] sometimes horrified... I was forced to give up my family life and so-called business life. And he brought me some way or other in preaching his gospel." — December 13, 1973, Los Angeles

## Question 4: Disappearance Day Ceremonies

Prabhupada describes or prescribes a consistent observance pattern for the disappearance day:

### Fasting and Pushpanjali

In the December 13, 1970 Indore Room Conversation, when Yamuna dasi reminded Prabhupada that December 15 was the disappearance day, he immediately gave instructions:

> Prabhupāda: "We shall fast up till twelve and then offer puṣpāñjali and then chant, as far as we can supply." — December 13, 1970, Indore

This is consistent with the general Vaishnava practice for disappearance days: fasting until noon, offering flowers (pushpanjali) to the picture or murti of the departed saint, and kirtan.

### Festival Observance

The December 16, 1970 Surat SB lecture was given the day after the disappearance day observance, and Prabhupada referenced it directly in the lecture:

> "Just like yesterday we had the disappearance day of His Divine Grace Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Goswami Prabhupāda. So we offered our respects and observed a festival. Āvirbhāva, Tirobhava." — December 16, 1970, Surat

### Temple Opening on the Disappearance Day

By 1977, the disappearance day had taken on institutional importance. The October and November 1977 Room Conversations show Prabhupada approving that the opening ceremonies of the new Hyderabad temple should begin on Bhaktisiddhanta's disappearance day (then calculated as December 29, 1977):

> Girirāja: "We fixed it for January 1st and begin the ceremonies on December 29th, which is Śrīla Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī's Disappearance Day."
> Prabhupāda: "Ah, that's nice." — November 5, 1977, Vrindavan

## Question 5: Recurring Themes Across Multiple Classes

Across all fourteen classes, the following themes appear repeatedly:

**1. Non-difference of appearance and disappearance (most consistent theme)**
Stated in every dedicated disappearance day lecture and both 1977 room conversations. The ācārya's disappearance is compared to the sun setting: not a termination but a movement out of our limited field of vision. The terms *nitya-līlā* (eternal pastimes) and *āvirbhāva/tirobhāva* are used consistently.

**2. The final letter of December 13, 1936 as the defining instruction**
Referenced in 1968, 1969, 1973, 1974, and 1976 lectures. The letter's content — "push our movement in English" — is treated as the founding mandate for ISKCON. Prabhupada returns to it year after year as the explanation for everything he has done.

**3. Personal mission rooted in the relationship with Bhaktisiddhanta**
Prabhupada uses the disappearance day occasion to narrate his own spiritual history: the 1922 first meeting, the initiation in 1933, the 1936 correspondence, his long wait as a householder, his retirement to Vrindavan, taking sannyasa under pressure from Bhaktiprajnana Kesava Maharaja (acting as instrument of Bhaktisiddhanta's will), and finally sailing to America in 1965. This autobiography appears in some form in nearly every class.

**4. Transfer of mission to disciples**
On each disappearance day occasion, Prabhupada explicitly passes the same instruction to his own disciples. The rhetorical structure is: "He told me; I am trying; now I am telling you." The 1968 lecture is the clearest: "I shall also request you to execute the same order through my will."

**5. The ācārya's presence through the paramparā**
Prabhupada consistently argues that Bhaktisiddhanta is not absent after his disappearance — he is present through the living chain of disciples. This is illustrated by Dr. Kapoor's observation in the 1974 Vrindavan lecture: "Although Bhaktisiddhānta Sarasvatī Gosvāmī Mahārāja has disappeared, he lives in Prabhupāda Bhaktivedanta Mahārāja."

**6. Emotional depth and genuine grief**
Despite the philosophical framing of non-difference, Prabhupada breaks into tears in the 1973 lecture while thanking his disciples. He quotes Narottama Das Thakura's song of separation. This combination of theological equanimity and personal grief is distinctive.

**7. The dreams of Bhaktisiddhanta as ongoing communication**
Mentioned in the 1968 Seattle lecture and the 1973 Los Angeles lecture. Prabhupada treated the recurring dream — in which Bhaktisiddhanta calls him to follow — as a genuine communication of the departed guru's will, not mere psychological experience.

**8. The founding of Back to Godhead magazine (1944) as first fulfilment of the instruction**
Referenced in the 1969 and 1974 lectures. Prabhupada started the magazine on February 7 (Bhaktisiddhanta's appearance day) in 1944 as an initial act of obedience to the December 1936 letter.

## Summary

Srila Prabhupada mentions the disappearance of his spiritual master Srila Bhaktisiddhanta Sarasvati Thakura in **fourteen audio classes** spanning 1968 to 1977. Four are dedicated disappearance day lectures; three are appearance day lectures with substantial treatment of the disappearance; seven are other contexts in which the topic arises. The corpus reveals a tightly consistent set of teachings: the disappearance is theologically equivalent to appearance (a Vaishnava never truly dies), the day is observed with fasting, pushpanjali, and kirtan, and the occasion always becomes an opportunity to narrate and transmit Bhaktisiddhanta's final instruction — to preach Caitanya Mahaprabhu's message in English — which Prabhupada received in a letter dated December 13, 1936, just two weeks before Bhaktisiddhanta's departure on December 31, 1936. That instruction is presented as the founding mandate for the entire ISKCON mission.
